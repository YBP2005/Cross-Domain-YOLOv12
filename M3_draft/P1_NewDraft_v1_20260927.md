# Budget, not domain — on the pairs we measure: how annotation budget moves a transfer gain, and can reverse its sign

Every number carries a verification tag. **✅CONFIRMED** means the number is traceable to a **recorded evidence path** in this project, of one of two kinds: **(a) recomputation from the released per-run tables**, for the §4 and §5 results; or **(b) an archived audit artifact**, for the §6 measurement-chain numbers, which come from perturbation, spectral, split and out-of-memory audits rather than from repeated training. Every ✅CONFIRMED number states **which seed set it uses**; where a cell has ten paired seeds and the reported figure rests on a subset, the subset is named. **No number in this draft carries ⚠PENDING**: the label axis, previously marked so, is reported in §4.6.*

---

## Abstract

Cross-domain fine-tuning of a lightweight detector loses target accuracy, and the standard response is to find a better transfer method. We take the opposite view: under a fixed annotation budget, the quantity that co-varies with whether a transfer gain survives is **the budget itself** — and on the graded cells we measure we do not identify it as the cause. On the same data pair and the same pipeline, extending fine-tuning from 100 to 200 epochs reduces the gain of a 5× peak learning rate without removing it: **+3.727 → +2.584 pp** (mAP50-95, ten of ten paired seeds lower), and on a second pair **+0.168 → −0.055 pp** ✅CONFIRMED (a third pair moves in the same direction but fails the registered significance level; §5). A same-pair four-point sweep reads **+3.656/+2.674/+1.666/+1.495 pp** at 30/50/100/200 epochs (endpoint difference **−2.161 pp**, t = −17.52) ✅CONFIRMED. We therefore treat annotation budget as **the measured independent variable** and report the protocol that makes the measurement auditable: paired seeds, a pre-registered multi-target replication with a frozen magnitude bar, and a three-way split that separates checkpoint selection from reporting. The replication fails its own magnitude bar on two of three pairs while succeeding in direction — we report that as the result. A companion audit shows that the first-order geometry diagnostics which the literature uses to explain such gains are **not measurable under our protocol at all**: signal-to-noise below 2 on all 18 measured cells, and a sign flip of the diagnostic under a change of the *reporting* checkpoint alone ✅CONFIRMED.

**Keywords:** cross-domain detection; annotation budget; budgeted fine-tuning; measurement protocol; evaluation audit

## 1. Introduction

A detector pretrained on a source domain and fine-tuned on a target domain loses accuracy on that target, and the literature has produced a large methodological response: alignment, adversarial adaptation, reconstruction, distillation (surveys: [1], [2]). That response shares one unexamined assumption — that the *method* is the object worth varying. In deployment the binding constraint is usually not the method but the **annotation budget**: a fixed number of labelled target images, a fixed number of fine-tuning epochs. We ask what that constraint does to the conclusions.

Our claim is narrow and testable: **on the pairs we measure, the sign of a transfer gain moves with the remaining budget** — the annotation-budget axis in the detection setting already carries that observation, reported by [18] (Zhang, Xu & Agrawal, arXiv:2606.30795) as the sign of an intervention's gain changing with the labelled budget, so what is new in this paper is not *that* a sign can change but **which** intervention moves it, under which pairing, and on what criterion the change is admitted (§2, §4.6). We do not claim to have identified budget *rather than* domain as the cause: on the cell carrying the largest gain the two covary, and the design that would separate them is not among those cells (§8). Concretely, a larger peak learning rate that helps a cross-domain pair at 20% labels and 100 epochs *stops helping* — and in one pair *starts hurting* — when the same pair is given 200 epochs, with the pretraining, the pipeline, the data split and the seed set held fixed ✅CONFIRMED.

Three consequences follow, and they organise the paper:

1. **The measured variable is budget** (§3, §4). We run the same pair at four budgets, paired by seed, and report the change in gain rather than the gain.
2. **The measurement must be auditable** (§5). Selection and reporting are separated by a three-way split; the confirmatory analysis is a pre-registered multi-target replication whose criterion (direction **and** significance **and** a magnitude bar) was frozen before its runs existed. It fails on magnitude for two of three pairs.
3. **The diagnostics usually offered for such gains do not survive the audit** (§6). The first-order geometry quantities used to explain transfer gains are trajectory-evaluated and evaluator-sensitive: below the noise floor on every measured cell, and sign-flipping under a change of the reporting checkpoint alone ✅CONFIRMED.

We do not propose a transfer method, and we claim no new bound or architecture. What we offer is a **measured, auditable statement about when a transfer gain exists at all**, together with the protocol and the audit that establish it.

## 2. Related work and positioning

**Cross-domain detection is a method literature.** [1] surveys unsupervised adaptation of detectors; [2] classifies the field into feature alignment, adversarial training, reconstruction and distillation. Both frame label scarcity as the problem a method must solve. We frame it as the quantity to be measured.

**Benchmarks in this area vary the distribution, not the budget.** The cross-domain document-detection suite [3] varies document domains; the RWDS suite [4] varies climate zone, disaster type and geography for domain generalisation. Both are data suites whose independent variable is the domain. Ours is the annotation budget.

**Evaluation audits are a recognised genre, and we follow its conventions.** LeakageBench [5] states its aim as separating model performance from information leakage and making a pass/warning/block decision auditable — explicitly *not* introducing a predictive algorithm. Datasheets and model cards [6][7] established documentation practice for datasets and models. The variance accounting of [8] is the methodological base we build on: variance from data sampling, initialisation and hyperparameter choice markedly affects benchmark conclusions, and their recommendation is to spend variation budget deliberately. Our paired-seed, multi-budget design is that recommendation applied to a detection fine-tuning campaign.

**Statistics are already used to predict transfer, and backbones are already compared.** Two strands of prior work bound what our §4.4 can claim as new. First, transfer difficulty has been predicted from **label statistics without training a model** — from the conditional entropy between source and target label assignments, shown to relate to the loss of the transferred model [9] — and, on the model side, the accuracy of a source model has been used to predict how well it transfers [10]; both are the same move we make in §9, where a **corpus** statistic is read as a moderator, and they differ from it in the statistic (conditional entropy; source accuracy), the dependent variable (transfer loss or hardness; accuracy) and the setting (classification). Second, the **backbone** has been treated as an isolated independent variable inside detection before: varying only the backbone on one dataset [11], and, closest to our protocol, holding the corpus, the split and *the number of epochs* fixed across six backbones before tuning hyperparameters [12].

**The budget dependence of the optimum is known; its *sign* is not.** The optimal learning rate moves with the training horizon and follows a scaling law [13]; the optimal effective rate moves with source–target similarity [14]; and fine-tuning instability has been attributed to the number of training iterations rather than to the amount of data [15]. **Two recent results report a reversal rather than a shift**: extending the *pretraining* budget can make models harder to fine-tune [16], and the preferred optimizer and its optimal hyperparameters change substantially — in places reversing — along the **overtraining** axis [17]. Closest to us in the detection setting, a sparse-label budget sweep over a source-free adaptation pipeline reports that **the sign of an intervention's gain changes with the labelled budget, and attributes it to the headroom that direct supervision leaves** [18]; **A separate line already argues that an apparent transfer gain need not come from transfer**: ImageNet pretraining speeds convergence without necessarily improving final accuracy [19], part of the transfer difference is over-parametrisation rather than feature reuse [20], fine-tuning only the batch-normalisation affine parameters matches full fine-tuning [21], a stronger training protocol can beat the pretraining source with detection included [22], and direct pretraining on the target corpus has been reported to beat ImageNet pretraining on COCO by 1.8 mAP [23]. We treat that line as the *context* of our §4 same-domain reading rather than as a competitor to it, because those works compare **pretraining sources or training components** for a single model, whereas what is measured here is the **sign of one strategy's paired gain** as the budget moves, with the pair, the pipeline and the seeds fixed. **The searches recorded with this submission did not return such a paired-gain measurement for detection** (the search record is reported with this submission, and Appendix O.3 states what the channels reached).

## 3. Design

**Setting.** A two-stage protocol: source-domain pretraining, then fine-tuning on a target domain with a fixed fraction of its labels, at a fixed number of epochs. Unless a cell says otherwise the detector is **YOLOv12n** [24] trained with the **shape-IoU** loss, scored by the mAP50-95 definition of [25] with the Ultralytics 8.4.120 `val` implementation as the concrete evaluator in every run; **2,241 of the 2,362 runs** in the released archive are on that backbone, the remainder being the two comparison generations (the residual 31 runs carry an unrecorded backbone and are excluded from the backbone comparison) of §4.4 (`yolo11n`, **76 runs**; `yolo26n`, **14 runs**), which are described there. Every comparison is same-budget, same-pipeline and paired by seed — **with one declared exception: in 20 of the 169 seed-gated cells the two arms' seed kinds are not the same** (Appendix P.8(iii)), and the §4.1 rows pair across families rather than within one (stated there). Gains are percentage points of mAP50-95. The corpora are the public detection datasets used as sources and targets throughout: SHWD [26] and SFCHD [27] for the helmet-detection cells, VisDrone [28], DOTA [29] and AI-TOD [30] for the aerial cells, with the remaining target corpora named where they first appear. Their per-corpus roles, split statistics and provenance are in Appendix L. **Fig. 1** schematises this two-stage pipeline and the four factors that are varied one at a time.

![Fig. 1](figures/F1_design_v2.png)

**Fig. 1.** Two-stage protocol schematic — source-domain pretraining, then fine-tuning on the target at a fixed label budget, read on the held-out test split at the validation-selected checkpoint. Below: the four factors varied one at a time (peak learning rate, epoch budget, label budget, released backbone); the two devices that make the protocol auditable (a three-way split that separates selection from reporting, and a criterion registered before the runs existed); and the audit applied to the first-order diagnostics. Column convention: the intervention and measurement results (§4.1, §4.2, §4.5, §4.6) are reported on the **held-out test split** (the corpus's own `test:` entry) — **except the two §4.2 rows and §4.1's T1-c, whose released `test:` key is the split that also selected the checkpoint, so for those the reported split is not an independent held-out one** (stated in Appendix M.3), whereas the two control subsections (§4.3, §4.4) are reported on the **validation-selected best checkpoint's validation reading**; §4.4's isolated backbone swap is where the two conventions put the **cross-backbone difference** on opposite sides of zero on the primary target’s corpus, although each backbone’s own gain is positive under both conventions. A third reading appears once, in §4.6: the same-domain contrast there is stated under the archive’s own endpoint rule rather than under either column. A fourth exception is the 2×2 sensitivity test of §5, which is reported on the **val** column — the same column as §4.3 and §4.4 (Supplementary N.4). **Of the two, the test column is this paper's primary column and the val-best column is its reported sensitivity**; the subsections that read the val-best column are the paper's **sensitivity group**, and their numbers are labelled as sensitivity wherever they are quoted. The same cell can differ between the two, and by more than a rounding step — on `shwd2sf→sfchd20` (100 epochs, thirteen paired seeds) the test column reads **+0.628 pp** while the best-checkpoint validation column reads **−0.307 pp**, a sign flip produced by the choice of column alone (§6(b)); every number in this paper states which of the two it uses, and the released per-run tables carry both columns. Sign convention: gains are always differenced as a **later or higher budget minus an earlier or lower budget**, so a **negative** difference is the direction the budget account predicts.

**Two axes, deliberately separated.**

| Axis | Operation | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **Budget in epochs** | re-run the same pair at 100 vs 200 epochs | whether a gain survives more optimisation |
| **Budget in labels** | re-run the same pair at 10/20/30/50% of target labels | whether a gain survives less supervision |

These are different independent variables and must not be conflated: one moves the *remaining* budget, the other the *available* labels.

**Replication and its criterion.** The multi-target replication was registered before any of its runs existed. It fixes three unsaturated target domains, a 20% label budget, one detector, and a criterion, quoted as registered, of **≥2/3 pairs meeting all three of: a paired significance level of p < 0.01, at least eight of ten seeds moving in the same direction, and an effect size of ≥ +0.30 pp**, under a Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q = 0.05 (registration, criterion and archive: supplement, Appendix N.1). The frozen magnitude bar is the part of that criterion we could not find precedent for in the literature.

**Three-way split.** Where the protocol is our own, the checkpoint is selected on one third of the corpus and read out on another, so selection and reporting never share data; re-keying a cell from its selection third to its reporting third moves the reading by −0.069 pp (p = 0.825) ✅CONFIRMED. **For two of the cited published cells the released `test:` key is also the split that selected the checkpoint, so for those the reported split is not an independent held-out one** (Appendix M.3); the split construction and the checkpoint-selection audit are in Appendix N.2.

## 4. Result: the gain falls, and its sign can change

**4.1 The intervention.** Two cells that carry the largest gains were re-run at 200 epochs, ten seeds per arm, same pretraining, same data, same pipeline; **the two rows are different pairs and their endpoints are paired across families (`t1c`/`t1b` against `mech`), which is stated because elsewhere this paper decomposes only within a single family** (Appendix P.10).

| Cell | 100 epochs | 200 epochs | Δ gain | seeds lower | paired t |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| `visdrone→dota15` | **+3.727 pp** | **+2.584 pp** | **−1.143 pp** | **10/10** | **−14.50** |
| `aitod→visdrone` | **+0.168 pp** | **−0.055 pp** | **−0.223 pp** | 9/10 | **−5.12** |

✅CONFIRMED (paired, per-seed recomputation). The direction is what a headroom account predicts — more budget, less remaining headroom, smaller gain — and it is the **opposite** of what "the strategy arm simply needed longer to converge" predicts. Three limits belong to this table: the intervention was decided **after** the results above (its analysis criteria were frozen before its runs, but the decision to run it was not); it covers **two cells at one budget interval**; and **the second row’s sign reversal is a test-column statement**. On the val-best column that cell runs **+1.152 pp at 100 epochs and +0.873 pp at 200**, so it falls without changing sign. The first row behaves the same way on that column (**+3.733 → +2.588 pp**, **Δ = −1.145 pp**, ten of ten seeds lower), so both rows fall without changing sign on the val-best column and the sign change occurs only in the test column.

**4.2 The curve.** A same-pair sweep at four budgets, ten paired seeds throughout:

| Budget | 30 ep | 50 ep | 100 ep | 200 ep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `smoke→SFCHD` | **+3.656** | **+2.674** | **+1.666** | **+1.495** |
| `shwd2sf` | **+0.812** | **+0.728** | **+0.694** | **+0.794** |

✅CONFIRMED. The first row gives the decreasing-gain prediction a **10-seed** basis, an endpoint difference of **−2.161 pp (t = −17.52, SD 0.391, ten of ten seeds lower)**, and a **monotone** descent at **every** step: 30→50 epochs **−0.98 pp** (ten of ten seeds lower), 50→100 **−1.01 pp** (ten of ten) and 100→200 **−0.17 pp** (eight of ten). Provenance of the tenth seed, stated because it is the one point in the row whose run is an **archived snapshot rather than a live directory**: the 30-epoch cell's `s50` arm pair completed all 30 epochs but was renamed as an interrupted snapshot and so never wrote its own test row; its reading was produced by a single re-evaluation under the driver's own selection rule (`best.pt`, the run's own `data` yaml, `split='test'`) and is carried by the released table. Its measured gain **+4.604 pp** matched the value recomputed from the released base table (**+4.600 pp**), which stores that cell to four decimals, and the reading, the two weight digests and the re-evaluation command are archived with this release. Every other point in the row rests on ten seeds from live run directories. Coverage in full: the `smoke→SFCHD` batch holds 133 runs, 112 of which carry a test reading (84%), and the uncovered remainder is archived intermediate material rather than lost runs. The second row does not: on that cell the fourth point **stops falling** (it sits 0.100 pp above the 100-epoch reading), and the 30-versus-200 endpoint difference is **−0.018 pp (t = −0.185, p = 0.86)** ✅CONFIRMED. We report the second row as a **bound, not a direction**. The **observed** spread of that cell's gain across the four budgets is **0.12 pp** (four point estimates between +0.694 and +0.812), which is a description of this sweep and not an inference; the **inferential** bound on a modulation is wider, because it must carry the seed variation: at ten paired seeds and the SD of the cell's own readings (0.321 pp), the 95% half-width is **±0.23 pp**, so what this row shows is **no detected change** at that scale: a modulation of about 0.23 pp or more would have been resolved, not 0.12 pp, and the half-width is not an equivalence claim. A modulation of 0.31 pp or more would have been detected at 80% power on the endpoint difference (paired *t* over the ten seeds, two-sided α = 0.05; SD of the 30-versus-200 per-seed differences = 0.308 pp ⇒ minimum detectable difference 0.306 pp) ✅CONFIRMED. **No other cell in the archive supports a bound at this standard**: across the 45 paired epoch-axis differences only **two** reach an MDE of 0.31 pp or below — this one, and a same-domain cell (`dota15→dota15`, 100→200 epochs, **−0.066 pp** at MDE 0.229 pp) — so a bounded null is a result this design can produce at one cross-domain cell, not a claim available archive-wide. **This row is where we differ most from the quantitative budget literature**: the scaling laws fitted to transfer report a *power law in the fine-tuning data size* with no sign change and no bound within the low-data regime the fit covers (the source's own §3.1 asks whether pre-training can harm performance) [31], and the reversal results are stated on the *pretraining* budget axis [16][17]. What is measured here is a **paired gain on one pair across the fine-tuning epoch axis**, with a **bounded null** on the second cell rather than another fitted curve.

**4.3 The saturated control.** On a target already saturated the same larger rate **damages** it. On this subsection's declared column, the validation-selected best-checkpoint column, `mask→mendeley` reads **−1.43 pp** (t = −6.69, p < 0.001, ten of ten seeds lower, ten paired seeds) ✅CONFIRMED — the sign the budget account predicts, obtained without any budget intervention. A **second, independent batch** on the same target (13 paired seeds) **does not reproduce that reading on the declared column**: it reads **+0.004 pp** (t = +0.02, seven of thirteen seeds negative), a null rather than a damage. The two batches agree only on the **held-out test** column, where the second gives **−1.17 pp** (t = −3.39) against the first's **−1.16 pp** (t = −3.03). We therefore report the reading as **carried by one batch on one column**. The per-batch, per-column table and the relation to §6(b)'s column sensitivity are in Appendix P.7.

**4.4 A second backbone — and, on the primary target's corpus, an isolated backbone swap.** The direction also holds on a second pipeline. On the `yolo11n`-based [32] `y11_shwd→sfchd` pair the gain is **+1.10 pp** (t = 9.44) at 30 epochs on the **validation-selected best-checkpoint column this subsection reads**, ten paired permutations from batch `y11` (the same reading on the test column is **+1.11 pp**, t = 9.60); At 100 epochs it is close to zero: **+0.19 pp** (t = 2.00) when the readings of the two batches that touch this pair are pooled — **only the ten seeds of batch `b2` are used, because the three permutations of batch `y11` are seeds 42–44, all of which `b2` already carries; the pool is therefore `n = 10` and not 13, and pooling across batches is used nowhere else in this paper (§4.5 and §4.6 pool only within a family), so we state it here** — **+0.32 pp** on batch `b2` alone (n = 10) and **−0.005 pp** on batch `y11` alone (n = 3, |t| = 0.10). The readings differ, because a batch is a training run of its own; what holds on **all three** is the qualitative reading we rely on — on this backbone the gain collapses between 30 and 100 epochs. The same pair also carries a **third backbone generation** (`yolo26n` [33], batch `y26`) on the same corpus and target: on the three permutations all three generations share (s42–s44), the val-best gains are **+0.92 / +1.27 / +2.64 pp** at 30 epochs and **+0.68 / −0.005 / +1.50 pp** at 100 epochs, and the test-column gains **+0.92 / +1.29 / +2.66 pp** and **+0.68 / +0.010 / +1.49 pp**, for `yolo12n` / `yolo11n` / `yolo26n` respectively — no generation is significantly negative — **the 100-epoch value itself sits inside its own 80%-power minimum detectable difference (|Δ| = +0.01 pp against MDE = 0.28 pp), so what carries the reading is the 30→100-epoch *difference* on that pair, not the 100-epoch point**. We read **no** ranking across generations from this, because the three sit in three different batches and the shared permutations number three. On these `sfchd` pairs the pretraining checkpoint fixes the backbone, so what they give is a replication across *(source, backbone)* combinations rather than an isolated architecture contrast, and we read no magnitude from them.

We can, however, isolate the backbone on the corpus of the primary target. A dedicated batch holds the same fine-tuning corpus (`dota15`, 20% of labels), the same epoch budget (100) and the same ten data-order permutations under two released backbones — `yolo11n` and `yolo12n` — so the backbone is the only factor that varies; the caveat is that a *released* checkpoint bundles an architecture with its own COCO weights, so this isolates the backbone **as shipped**, not the architecture alone. The learning-rate gain is present under **both**, in **both** column conventions: **+3.75 / +3.51 pp** on the test column (§4.1–§4.2's convention) and **+7.57 / +8.66 pp** on the val-best column (§4.3–§4.4's), with **ten of ten permutations positive in every one of the four arm × backbone cells**. The cross-backbone difference in gain is **−0.24 pp** (t = −1.59, p = 0.15) on the test column and **+1.10 pp** (t = +2.08, p = 0.07) on the val-best column — small against the gain itself, and significant in neither. ✅CONFIRMED: the gain is **not detectably different** across the two released backbones on this corpus and budget; so we detect no change in what the two released backbones do here, not a claim that backbone choice is irrelevant in general. The practical reading of this is a **conditional** one,: a comparison that fixes the corpus, the split, the epoch budget and the seed set, and varies only the released backbone, is **already** present in the detection literature [11][12], so what is new in this paragraph is not the isolation; it is that we report the **budget gain itself under two backbones** and test the **difference between the two gains**, rather than the two backbones' accuracies. The released weights are also a known confound: a checkpoint bundles an architecture with its own pretraining, and work that separates the two effects exists [34], which is why we scope the reading to the backbone **as shipped**. Two limits travel with it the swap covers **one corpus at one epoch budget**, and the permutations are **data-order permutations with the torch seed fixed**, so ten of ten speaks to permutation robustness rather than to ten independent retrainings. What remains out of reach is generality beyond this corpus and budget, not the backbone comparison itself.

**4.5 How general is this?** The epoch axis and the label axis are not the same instrument, and the whole run archive says so. We swept **every** analysable pair–batch group rather than the four reported above — 61 paired endpoint differences drawn from 22 groups (14 independent pairs) — and then applied the **same seed gate we use everywhere else**: a difference counts only if it rests on **ten paired seeds, on at least five with a unanimous sign, or reaches |t| ≥ 4**. Of the 45 epoch-axis differences **20** clear that gate and **17 of them (85%) are negative**; one pair of `dota15→dota15` families (`g3cleanb`, `g3ext`) appears twice in those twenty because the same experiment is recorded under two run directories, identical seed by seed and column by column, so the count is 17 of 19 (**89%**) once that duplicate is removed; of the 16 label-axis differences **8** clear it and **only 2 (25%) are negative — three quarters positive** ✅CONFIRMED (each entry uses the seeds available at both endpoints). The two axes therefore carry **opposite majority signs**, and the dissociation survives the gate rather than depending on the ungated entries. **Where the epoch-axis gain falls, the archive says which arm moved**: over the 55 (pair, family, label budget) comparisons with two epoch budgets and all four arms present, the dominant account is **baseline catch-up — the baseline arm improves more than the intervention arm — in 47**, against **intervention-arm degradation in 5** and **both arms falling in 3** (the identity and the per-comparison table are in Appendix P.10).

**The sign is not merely counted; it is predicted out of sample, and it fails in three named places.** A count and a prediction are different objects, so we test the stronger one: using only each pair–family's **lowest label budget** to fix the predicted sign of its epoch-axis response, and holding out **every higher label budget** — different target subsets, and therefore different runs rather than a re-draw of the same cell the held-out sign is predicted correctly on **21 of 25** slices, and in **4 of the 6** pair–families that carry all five label budgets the sign is unanimous across all five ✅CONFIRMED. The same exercise along the other axis, where the label-axis sign measured at 30 epochs predicts the sign at 100 epochs, holds in **7 of 7** pair–families. That count uses, for each epoch budget, the widest label-budget tier that family itself carries; requiring instead the tiers common to both epoch budgets gives **6 of 7**. Two limits belong to the numbers and we state them with it: the **independent units are the families, not the slices** (there are seven of them, so 21 of 25 is not a percentage of independent predictions), and the exercise was decided after the archive existed, although its split follows the budget order and is not chosen against outcomes. **The count must therefore not be read as an 84% independent-prediction accuracy**: aggregated the way the units are independent, the reading is **four of the six families that carry all five label budgets agreed with the sign fixed at their lowest slice across all five** (the per-family table and the three named sign-reversing exceptions are in Appendix P.6), and **no permutation p at the level of the seven families is attached to the count of 21 of 25**, so that count is a per-family description of the archive rather than a test; what can be tested at that level is the sign agreement among the six families that carry all five label budgets — **all six agree on the sign fixed at their lowest slice, a two-sided sign test at $p = 0.016$** — which is the strongest form the family-level statement takes here. The failures are named rather than averaged away: `mafa→mask_clean` at 10% of labels reads +0.057 pp while its 30% slice reads −0.547 pp, `shwd2sf→sfchd`/`a0` reads −0.320 at 20% and +0.097 at 40%, and `shwd→sfchd`/`a0` reads −0.200 at 10% against +0.233 at 40% and +0.327 at 50% — three of them sign-reversing, though under the $\gamma(n)$ convention of Appendix P **no single family has both of its reversed slices above its own minimum detectable difference**, so the falsification condition is not yet instantiated and the claim is "four of the six families that carry all five label budgets unanimous, with named exceptions" and not "the sign is a property of the pair".

The cleanest single instance is the one family where we filled the **whole** budget grid at ten paired seeds — `visdrone→dota15`, five label budgets crossed with two epoch budgets. Along the **label axis** the gain rises steeply and then saturates: **+1.42 pp** from 10% to 20% of labels (t = 16.2, ten of ten seeds), **+0.52 pp** from 20% to 30% (t = 4.0), **+0.17 pp** from 30% to 40% (t = 2.1), and **−0.14 pp** from 40% to 50% (t = −1.8, not significant). Along the **epoch axis** the gain *falls* at four of the five budgets — **−1.03, −1.50, −1.32 and −0.91 pp** at 20%, 30%, 40% and 50% of labels, **ten of ten seeds lower in each** (t = −8.1 to −15.1) — and is **not detectable at 10%** (−0.12 pp, t = −1.1, seven of ten). We report that 10%-label slice as a **null, not an opposite**: the dissociation on this family is carried by the four budgets where all ten seeds agree on the negative sign, while the saturating label axis says that the *first* tenth of the labels is where the gain lives.

We do not claim universality. Restricting the sweep to the tier that survives our own seed gate — a difference backed by **ten paired seeds, or by at least five with a unanimous sign** — leaves a handful of cells, and **both signs survive it**: `pcb→neu_det` **loses 1.4–9.8 pp** of gain going from 30 to 100 epochs (ten of ten seeds at three of its five label budgets and nine of ten at the other two; t = −3.6 to −9.8) and **up to 10.7 pp** going from 10% to 50% of labels (ten of ten at 30 epochs, t = −9.2; 2.4 pp with seven of ten at 100 epochs); `smoke→SFCHD` loses **2.16 pp** from 30 to 200 epochs (ten of ten seeds, t = −17.5); but `visdrone→dota15` **gains 1.19 pp** going from 10% to 50% of labels (ten of ten, t = +14.1), and `fsin→smoke` gains at **all five** label budgets (+0.47 to +1.17 pp, three of three seeds each) ✅CONFIRMED. The sign of a budget response is therefore a property of the **pair**, not a law of budget: the same archive that makes the epoch axis reliably negative also contains pairs where more labels help. We accordingly report the epoch axis (§4.1–§4.2) as this paper's result and treat the label axis as a **separate, pair-dependent** instrument rather than a second copy of it.

**4.6 The label axis, reported separately.** The annotation-label axis is a result of this paper, reported as a **separate instrument with its own sign**. Two pairs now carry a **complete five-budget by two-epoch grid at ten paired seeds per cell**. On `pcb→neu_det` the label axis is **strongly negative**: the gain falls by **10.71 pp** from 10% to 50% of labels at 30 epochs (ten of ten seeds lower, t = −9.2) and by **2.39 pp** at 100 epochs (seven of ten, t = −3.1). On `visdrone→dota15` it is **positive at both epochs** — **+1.98 pp** at 30 epochs and **+1.19 pp** at 100 epochs, ten of ten seeds each (t = +14.9 and +14.1) — and it **saturates**: the first step alone contributes **+1.42 pp** (10% to 20% of labels, ten of ten) while the next three contribute **+0.52, +0.17 and −0.14 pp**. Across the archive, of the label-axis differences that clear our seed gate **six of eight are positive**, against **seventeen of twenty negative** on the epoch axis (twenty entries contain one duplicated directory pair; deduplicated, 17 of 19 = 89%) ✅CONFIRMED. **The label axis is, however, where our own power audit bites hardest: several of the cells behind that count have |Δ| below their 80%-power minimum detectable difference (§9), so the count is a description of the archive rather than a resolvable per-cell claim.** **The closest published work to this axis is a sparse-label budget sweep in source-free adaptation, which already reports that an intervention's gain can go from positive at 1% of labels to negative at larger budgets, and attributes it to the headroom that direct supervision leaves** [18]. The difference is one of role and of criterion rather than of object: there the budget selects between methods and the reported quantity is a plugin's difference from an anchor; here the budget is the measured independent variable, the reported quantity is a **paired** gain on the same pair, pipeline and seed set, and the label axis is read through a criterion frozen before the runs existed.

**The gain is not a property of transfer.** The archive also contains **same-domain** controls — pretraining and fine-tuning on the *same* corpus (`dota15→dota15`, `aitod→aitod20`, `visdrone→visdrone`, `mask→mask`, `dota→dota`) — and their loss-plane gains are **also positive on average**: **+0.27 pp** over 17 cells (median +0.65, 29% negative, 16 of 17 reaching |t| ≥ 2), against **+1.10 pp** over 130 cross-domain cells (median +0.94, 23% negative) ✅CONFIRMED. That same-domain average is stated under the archive’s own endpoint rule; under the clean three-way protocol the same seven within-domain corpora read **six negative and one null** at 30 epochs (−0.03 to −4.38 pp), which is a different reading of the same corpora rather than a contradiction of it, and the per-seed values are in Appendix M.5. The gain therefore does **not require a domain gap**. We state that much as a measurement; the stronger reading — that the gain reflects the **loss function** specifically — is a candidate explanation rather than a measured result, because the loss axis is not independently manipulated anywhere in this paper and the same-domain and cross-domain sets are not matched in pair or budget. In this respect the comparison is qualitative, because it is what the title's "budget, not domain — on the pairs we measure" predicts, and the qualifier carries more weight after §5's cross-corpus test than it did before it, and the qualifier in that title is what this reading can and cannot support; we draw no magnitude from it. Two things we do **not** claim for this reading. It is not the first comparison of same-domain and cross-domain pretraining on detection — direct pretraining on the target corpus has been reported to beat ImageNet pretraining on COCO, by 1.8 mAP and at more than 11× the convergence speed [23] — and neither is the *deflationary* move ours: that ImageNet pretraining speeds convergence without necessarily improving final accuracy [19], that part of the transfer difference is over-parametrisation rather than feature reuse [20], that fine-tuning only the batch-normalisation affine parameters matches full fine-tuning [21], and that a stronger training protocol beats the pretraining source, detection included [22], are all established. What is specific here is the **contrast** rather than the source: the claim concerns the sign of one strategy's paired gain, not which pretraining corpus is better, and the same-domain set is reported with its own distribution (17 cells, 29% negative) rather than as a single comparison.

**A second, independent test, and it does not confirm the reading.** The registered test holds the domain pair fixed and varies the label budget at one epoch budget; it therefore measures direction and magnitude but not whether the budget *sets the sign*. ## 6. Result: the diagnostics that should explain this do not survive measurement

Most accounts of a transfer gain reach for a first-order geometry quantity — a gradient-alignment score, a worst-direction loss increment, a sharpness read — as the explanatory variable. **That family has already been questioned on its own terms**: reparametrization-invariant sharpness tracks training parameters such as the learning rate rather than generalization, and the relation can invert out of distribution [35]; the choice of measure is data-dependent [36]; and flatness reads are not invariant to reparametrization [37][38]. **Our contribution here is therefore not a further correlation study but a decision procedure and a measurement**, and the per-paper differences are tabulated in the supplement (Appendix O.1). This section is an **audit**, not a model.

**(a) The quantities are below the noise floor.** Under a signal-to-noise criterion fixed before the data existed, the alignment proxies fail on **every** measured cell: **18/18 cells with SNR < 2** (band 0.76–1.47) ✅CONFIRMED, while the observed fluctuation between repeated evaluations is ≈2.3× — the same order as the gate itself.

**(b) They are evaluator-sensitive, and their ranking flips.** The alignment *ranking* changes with the evaluator variant used to compute it (the flip chain of the supplement, Appendix H.1) ✅CONFIRMED. The cleanest instance is a **reporting** change rather than an evaluator change: for one cell, with the runs, the seeds and the fine-tuning untouched, **only the column that is reported** decides the sign — `shwd2sf→sfchd20` reads **+0.628 pp on the held-out test column** and **−0.307 pp on the validation-selected best-checkpoint column** (this is the `shwd2sf`-family cell, not the separate `shwd→sfchd`/`a0` pair that also appears in this archive) — a sign flip produced by the reporting rule alone ✅CONFIRMED.

**(c) What they are not.** The customary target-domain "flatness" read is not a curvature measurement at this scale: the worst-direction increment scales with the perturbation radius at an exponent of **0.56–0.81**, where a curvature-dominated read would require ≈2 ✅CONFIRMED. By contrast, source-side stationary spectral reads are stable across strategies (253–284, ±6%), which is the discrimination we rely on: **stationary quantities can carry claims; trajectory-evaluated ones cannot.**

We take (a)–(c) as a boundary on **what this protocol can measure**, not as a claim that the family is worthless in general — the grounds for scepticism are older than this paper [35][36], and what is new here is only the measurement: if the diagnostic is below the floor and its sign depends on the reporting rule, then a claim of the form "the gain is explained by geometry $X$" is not measurable under this protocol, whatever the value of $X$. This is the part of the paper that most needs the audit genre's conventions, and we have written it that way: what was measured, what was not, and what would have to change for the claim to become available.

**The measurement-hierarchy proposition, stated so that it can be refuted.** The three readings above are instances of one statement, and we state it here in the form we can defend. At a checkpoint $\theta$, perturbing along a unit direction $u$ at the protocol's relative radius, the worst-direction increment is a two-term expansion: a **first-order** term proportional to $\rho$ and carrying the local slope $\langle\nabla R_D(\theta),u\rangle$, and a **second-order** term proportional to $\rho^2$ and carrying curvature. Which term speaks is governed by one ratio, $r_D(\theta,\rho) = \|\nabla R_D(\theta)\| / (\|H_D(\theta)\|\,\rho\|\theta\|)$, the local slope measured in units of the local curvature scale at the operational radius. In the aligned case with a constant Hessian the two terms stand in the ratio $1/(2r_D)$, so a **small $r_D$ is curvature-dominated** and a **large $r_D$ is slope-dominated**, crossing over at $r_D=\frac12$; taking the usual "four times" and "twice" conventions puts the two regime labels at $r_D\le\frac1{16}$ and $r_D\ge1$ respectively — $\frac12$ is the point of equal magnitude, not a sufficiency threshold, so the two conventions do not share an endpoint. **Those two numbers are conventions, not consequences of H6/H8**: the hypotheses bound $\|H\|$ from above but give no positive lower bound on $u^{T}Hu$ for the direction the trajectory actually takes, an arbitrary $u$ can be orthogonal to the gradient, and the intermediate-point Hessian is not bounded from the base-point one here, so no threshold follows without the direction, positive-curvature and remainder conditions stated with it. **Statement** (footprint **H6** and **H8** of Appendix A): that dichotomy holds, and a finite-SGD checkpoint carrying a non-negligible residual gradient will generally produce a *slope-dominated* read, whose value is then a function of where the trajectory stopped rather than of the endpoint's curvature. **We do not measure $r_D$**: the numerator is a gradient norm at fine-tuned endpoints, which this protocol does not record, so what we contribute is the derivation and the regime judgement, not a located boundary. The derivation, both thresholds, the placement argument and the list of what the statement does **not** claim are in Appendix A-bis.

Three of our readings bear on the placement. The **ε-scaling exponents of 0.56–0.81 (median 0.59)** are an effective two-point slope over a finite radius interval, and they sit far below the $\approx2$ that curvature domination would require, so curvature domination is excluded at those cells; an exponent near $2$ would place them in the curvature branch and would falsify the placement we report, on its own and without any further condition. The **SNR floor (18/18 cells $<2$, band 0.76–1.47)** is a failure of the gate for *using* the diagnostic as an outcome predictor, which is a different question from first- against second-order, and the two are not pooled. The **evaluator and reporting sensitivity** of §6(b) is a third and independent obstacle: the ranking flips with the evaluator, and in the clearest instance the sign of the reported gain flips with the **reporting column alone**, runs and seeds untouched. What we do not claim is impossibility: a larger gradient batch or a variance-reduced estimator changes the residual gradient that $r_D$ is built from, and with it the regime, as Appendix H.1 states; the statement is about this protocol and this estimator.

## 7. Statistics and internal validity

**Scope of inference.** Two **units of inference** are used — the **cell**, keyed by (pair, family, label budget, epoch budget) and read as a paired mean over seeds, and the **contrast**, a difference between two budgets' cells or two backbones' gains — and the statistical instruments attached to them are not interchangeable. **One claim carries a nominal error rate**: the pre-registered criterion of §5, with its registered significance level p < 0.01, its directional requirement and its Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q = 0.05, and it met them on one of the three pairs. The family correction of this section, the seed gate of §4.5 and the resolution audit of §9 are a **description**, an **eligibility filter** and a **resolution statement** respectively, none of which carries an error rate of its own. **Two extended clean-protocol level effects do pass our retrospective family correction and the resolution screen at once** — the eleven- and thirteen-seed cells of Appendix F.3, whose adjusted values are $q=0.0159$ and whose $|\bar\Delta|$ exceeds its own MDE — while **no budget-response claim has a prospectively controlled joint guarantee**, and none is presented as if it did; Appendix P.5 sets out the scope of each instrument and the audit's own limits.

**What inference rests on.** The paired tests at the cell level (ten seeds), the fresh-seed subset, and the permutation tests. The family-level correction is reported as **robustness only**: the roster of cells was enumerated after the outcomes were seen, so it cannot control the false-discovery rate of the family that produced the selection. The frozen effect-size rule decides only which arms were extended and is **never** an inferential threshold.

**Where selection is visible, and how we handled it.** For the cells whose selection and reporting splits coincide, the reported level is a maximum over epochs on the split that selected the checkpoint. The three-way split in §3 moves that reading by −0.069 pp (p = 0.825) ✅CONFIRMED; under the clean three-way protocol the two headline cells read **+1.434 / +0.507 pp** at ten seeds (SD 0.383 / 0.488 pp, paired t = 11.84 / 3.29, sign-flip permutation p = 0.0020 / 0.0098, ten of ten and nine of ten seeds positive) ✅CONFIRMED, and **+1.409 / +0.628** at eleven and thirteen seeds (SD 0.372 / 0.489 pp, t = 12.55 / 4.63, p = 0.000977 / 0.001221, eleven of eleven and twelve of thirteen positive) ✅CONFIRMED — the extension to eleven and thirteen was undertaken **with the ten-seed values already known**, and that provenance is part of the reading.

**Audit of the measurement chain.** Five classes of infrastructure incident were found and are reported with their defensive rules (the supplement carries the narrative and the timeline, Appendix E): seed replay in two independent instances, a pipeline-version offset, a silent module-layer dropout under a framework rebuild, and a dead-layer insertion. One further class affects numbers rather than code: the framework's task-aligned assigner catches a CUDA out-of-memory error and silently falls back to CPU. A full-log scan bounded its effect at a median **−0.0006 pp** over 20 cells against a data-order σ̂ of **0.166–0.190 pp** ✅CONFIRMED, and the origin (process concurrency) was removed at source.

## 8. Discussion

**What the paper establishes.** Under a fixed annotation budget, a transfer gain is not a property of the pair alone: on the same pair, with everything else fixed, **extending the fine-tuning budget reduces the gain and can reverse its sign** (§4.1), the reduction is visible as a curve on one cell and bounded to nothing on another (§4.2), a saturated target is damaged by the same larger rate (§4.3), and a second architecture reproduces the same ordering — a larger gain at the short budget and a smaller one at the long budget — at a reduced magnitude (§4.4); and across the whole run archive the two budget axes carry **opposite majority signs**, with the sign of a response pair-specific rather than universal (§4.5); the **label axis is itself a measured result**, rising on one pair and falling on another (§4.6). The pre-registered test fails its own magnitude bar while succeeding in direction on all three targets (§5). And the geometry diagnostics that would ordinarily explain this are not measurable under the protocol at all (§6).

**What it does not establish.** We do not separate the two candidate moderators. On the cell carrying the largest gain, remaining budget headroom and measured domain shift are at their extremes together, so no measurement here attributes the effect to one rather than the other; the experiment that would separate them — shift held fixed while the budget varies, at several levels — is not among these cells. We also do not establish the effect beyond lightweight detectors on the pairs measured, and its counts are where our own power audit bites hardest, so the axis is carried as a description of the archive rather than as a resolvable claim for any one of its cells.

**A two-term account of the sign, and the experiment that would separate it.** The pattern admits a two-term description. Write the gain of the strategy arm over the baseline at budget $B$ as $G(B)=U(B)-M(B)$: $U(B)$ is the **usable headroom** the optimisation path leaves at that budget — the account assumes $U\ge0$, which is a definitional commitment about what "headroom" means and not one of its measurements — so $U$ falls as $B$ grows because a longer path has less room left to improve, and $M(B)$ is the **mismatch cost** the same extra budget buys, so $M$ rises because a longer or better-labelled fit is also a fit tied more closely to the split it was selected on. Then $G'(B)=U'(B)-M'(B)$, and with $U'<0<M'$ this is **negative at every budget on the epoch axis**: the two terms move in opposite directions, so on that axis $G$ falls monotonically and what can change sign is **$G$ itself**, once at most, and only if the pair starts with $G>0$ at the lowest budget measured. The description is a definition, not an estimate, and the two parts of it that carry content are the *directions* it assigns to the two terms and what it says about **shape**. The directions it assigns make no prediction about how many cells reverse: that count is set by where each pair's $G$ sits at the lowest budget, which the description does not fix. The shape it does commit to is the one below — a label axis that rises then flattens while the epoch axis falls — and the archive's endpoint counts are consistent with it rather than evidence for it: 17 of 20 gated epoch-axis endpoint differences are negative (twenty entries contain one duplicated directory pair; deduplicated, 17 of 19 = 89%) and 2 of 8 gated label-axis ones. On shape, the description predicts that on a pair whose headroom is still partly unspent the label axis rises and then flattens — $+1.42$, $+0.52$, $+0.17$, $-0.14$ pp across the four label steps of §4.6, the last step already inside the noise and reported there as not significant — while the epoch axis falls, at every label budget where it is detected, by $-1.03$, $-1.50$, $-1.32$ and $-0.91$ pp and not at all at the 10% slice ($-0.12$ pp, $t=-1.1$). Both readings are what the description asks for, but we do not present them as a confirmation: with $U$ and $M$ unmeasured the description can accommodate either sign at a given budget by placing the crossing, so the quantity it predicts is the **dissociation of the two axes and the flattening of each**, not the sign of any particular cell. The two rows of §4.2 are the same object seen from two sides: on one cell the sum is monotone across 30–200 epochs, and on the other it is flat within $0.12$ pp, which the description reads as a pair whose $U$ is already spent at 30 epochs, so that no budget in the swept range can move its sign.

**What this account is not.** It is not fitted and it does not separate its own terms: $U$ and $M$ are **not measured independently anywhere in this paper** — $M$ is never measured at all — no coefficient is estimated, and the account is therefore a **description rather than a model**. One reading of $M$ is not merely unmeasured but **excluded by the archive**, and we state the exclusion because it is what keeps the description from being vacuous: if $M$ were a source–target **mismatch** cost, then on a **same-domain** cell it would vanish and $G=U$ would have to be positive, whereas the same-domain controls contain cells that are clearly negative — `aitod→aitod20` at 30 epochs reads **−1.195 pp** (t = −7.85) and `mask→mask20` at 30 epochs **−2.027 pp** (t = −5.44) ✅CONFIRMED — both are family `r10`, dataset `*_3way`, ten paired seeds, read on the validation-selected best-checkpoint column, which is the column this subsection reads (Appendix M.5). $M$ is therefore **more naturally read as a budget-side** term than as a function of the domain gap, consistent with the loss-function reading of §4.6, and the description is a statement about the two budget axes rather than about domain distance. It is falsifiable in one specific way, and we state the test rather than claim it is passed: **on the epoch axis, a cross-budget difference of $G$ that is positive and above its own minimum detectable difference, at a budget large enough that the path's remaining headroom is already spent, would falsify the account there** — the test is decided by that measurable difference alone. Running that test needs the shift held fixed while the budget varies at several levels because the confound is structural, not a matter of sample size. Until it is run, the account's content is that it makes the **dissociation** of §4.5 and §4.6 a consequence of one crossing rather than a coincidence, and it locates the paper's mechanism claim one step short of a measurement.

**Why the two axes need not agree, stated as a distinction rather than as a second account.** The two budgets are different functionals of the same campaign, and the difference is worth naming because it is what makes §4.5's dissociation expected rather than surprising. The **epoch** budget moves along a path at fixed data: the label subset, the fitting target and the sequence of mini-batches are the same object at both endpoints, and the later endpoint differs only in how far the optimiser has travelled. The **label** budget changes the data the path is run on: the target subset, the number of gradient steps per epoch and the fitting target all move together, so the later endpoint is a different optimisation problem rather than a longer run of the same one. A quantity defined on the path therefore inherits the path's decay, and a quantity defined on the data inherits the supervision available; that is the whole of the asymmetry, and it is a statement about the two instruments rather than about the pairs. It is also, explicitly, **not** a law that a given pair must satisfy in a given direction: the gated counts are 17 of 20 negative on the epoch axis (twenty entries contain one duplicated directory pair; deduplicated, 17 of 19 = 89%) against 2 of 8 on the label axis, and the archive contains label-axis responses of both signs, `pcb→neu_det` falling by **10.71 pp** from 10% to 50% of labels where `visdrone→dota15` rises by **1.98 pp**, so we read those counts as the majority direction of two different instruments and not as a prediction for the next pair (§4.6, §9). One relation between the two axes is **not** established and we do not state it: whether the label axis's saturation point and the epoch axis's zero crossing are the same quantity seen twice. The grids that would settle it do not exist in one object — §4.6 has complete five-budget by two-epoch grids on two pairs, but only on `visdrone→dota15` are the **label** and **epoch** axes both filled at ten seeds across the same grid, and the crossing on the other pair is not located by anything we measured.

**Why we think the audit belongs in the paper rather than in the appendix.** A measurement paper's contribution is only as good as its falsifiability, and the three things that most threaten ours are all visible: selection (handled by the three-way split, and quantified), multiplicity (handled by reporting the correction as robustness and saying why it cannot be a guarantee), and measurement floor (handled by §6, which removes a whole family of explanations).

## 9. Limitations

* **Two cells** carry the epoch-axis intervention, at **one budget interval**; the intervention was decided after the primary results.
* The **label axis** (10/20/30/50% of target labels) is reported separately in §4.6, on two pairs with a complete five-budget grid at ten paired seeds. Its **sign is pair-specific** — `pcb→neu_det` falls by 10.7 pp from 10% to 50% of labels while `visdrone→dota15` rises by 2.0 pp — so we report the axis but not a direction for it in general.
* **Direction without magnitude.** The replication's magnitude bar is met by one of three pairs; the effect is smaller than the criterion on the other two.
* **Backbone scope.** The *direction* replicates across backbones, in two different senses: on a second *(source, backbone)* combination (the 100-epoch gain is **+0.19 pp**, t = 2.00, where the 30-epoch reading on the same pair is +1.10 pp), and — on the primary target's own corpus — under an **isolated backbone swap**, where the gain is present with **ten of ten seeds positive in all four arm × backbone cells** and the cross-backbone difference in gain is **−0.24 pp (t = −1.59)** on the test column and **+1.10 pp (t = +2.08)** on the val-best column. What does **not** transfer is the *magnitude*, and the isolated swap covers **one corpus at one epoch budget**, with **data-order permutations** rather than independent retrainings. We therefore scope the magnitude claim to the pipeline measured and report the direction as the replicated part.
* **Loss-axis consistency check.** On the three groups where all four legs exist — the gain's two arms under `shapeiou` and under a swapped loss (`sns`, `sns`, `pws`) — the gain's **sign is unchanged**, and swapping the loss moves the gain by **at most 0.37 pp on the val-best column and 0.41 pp on the test column**, with no group reaching |t| ≥ 2 and six to seven paired permutations per group. We report this as a **consistency check, not a result**: the groups are few and their permutation counts fall below the ten-paired-seed bar we apply elsewhere, so it is evidence that the gain is **not specific to `shapeiou`**, not a measurement of a loss effect.
* **Pair dependence.** Neither budget axis is universal. Across the archive's 22 groups, of the endpoint differences that clear our seed gate **85% of the epoch-axis ones are negative while only 25% of the label-axis ones are** — and both signs survive inside the gate (e.g. `visdrone→dota15` gains 1.19 pp from 10% to 50% of labels, ten of ten seeds, while `pcb→neu_det` loses 10.7 pp on the same axis). What replicates is the *mechanism* — budget moves the gain — not the sign, which is pair-specific.
* **The audit's own floor.** §6 concludes that a family of diagnostics is unusable here; that conclusion is itself bounded by the perturbations and evaluator variants we tried, and it is stated as a floor, not as a claim that no such diagnostic can work.


* **Resolvability (our own power audit).** Every cell was scored against its own 80%-power minimum detectable difference (paired *t*, α = 0.05): **43 of 147 cells have |Δ| below their MDE** — **32 of 73 at three seeds (44%)** against **11 of 74 at more than three seeds (15%)**; the count is about single-budget **levels**, not the **differenced** quantities §4 lives on, and the headline cells are unaffected (the §4.2 row's endpoints at **13.9× and 13.4×** their level MDE, the two primary §4.1 readings at **4.6× and 1.4×**). **Detail, per-cell table and scope: Appendix Q.1.**
* **Resolvability is itself a function of the reporting column**, and **the metric is a second reporting convention**: switching columns changes the verdict on about **11%** of gated cells, and mAP50 and mAP50-95 disagree in sign on **4 of the 15** long-budget cells (**Fig. S3**, Table T12), which is why mAP50-95 is the primary metric and why a sign reading here belongs to a stated convention. **Detail: Appendix Q.2–Q.3.**
* **The same audit as one criterion.** The audit's threshold and §4.2's MDE are one judgement at two sample sizes: a cell is resolvable **iff** $|\bar\Delta|/\hat\sigma \ge \gamma(n)$ with $\gamma(3) = 3.10$, $\gamma(10) = 0.99$; the 44%-against-15% contrast is **not** mechanical in $n$ (median $|\bar\Delta|/\hat\sigma$ is **4.37** at three seeds against **3.03** at ten), and ten seeds is a **retrospective account, not an ex-ante rule**. **Detail: Appendix Q.4.**
* **What the audit covers, and what it does not.** It is not the **seed gate** of §4.5: a cell can pass the gate and fail the audit, and only the right to read its **magnitude** is withdrawn (Appendix Q.5).
* **Not fully separated.** Headroom and shift covary on the graded cells, and the one moderator that tracks the sign — **annotation density** (three sparsest corpora negative, eleven of eleven and eight of eight; two densest **+2.16 and +0.95 pp**; $r\approx0.34$; `neu_det` the clear exception) — is **confounded with the pair**, so it is reported as a **candidate** moderator and as a signpost for a design that varies density deliberately; the quantity read here is a countable **annotation** statistic, not the **beam-density difference between collectors** that detection transfer already treats as a source of the domain gap and removes [39]. **Detail: Appendix Q.6.**
---

## 10. Conclusion

On the pairs we measure, the quantity that co-varies with whether a transfer gain survives in this setting is the remaining budget, not the domain; **we do not identify it as the cause**, and §4.5 gives the pairs where the sign goes the other way. Holding the pair, the pretraining and the pipeline fixed and moving only the fine-tuning budget takes a 5× learning-rate gain from **+3.727 pp to +2.584 pp** on one pair, a change of −1.143 pp that clears its own minimum detectable difference by 4.6× with ten of ten seeds moving down, and from **+0.168 pp to $-$0.055 pp** on another. On that second pair we state the resolvable quantity rather than the level: the 200-epoch reading of −0.055 pp is **not** distinguishable from zero at our power on its own, since its minimum detectable difference is 0.141 pp and it sits at 0.39× of that threshold, whereas the **change** across the budget, −0.223 pp, clears its own minimum detectable difference by 1.6× with nine of ten seeds moving down.

**Strengths.** The measured variable is the budget itself. On the same pair, pipeline and seed set, extending the fine-tuning budget cuts the gain and can reverse its sign (§4.1); the reduction appears as a curve on one cell and as a bounded null on another (§4.2); the label axis carries a sign of its own that is pair-specific (§4.6); and across the whole archive the two axes carry opposite majority signs that survive our own seed gate (§4.5). The protocol is what makes each of these checkable rather than asserted: paired seeds, a three-way split that keeps selection and reporting apart, a criterion registered before its runs existed with a frozen magnitude bar, and a confirmatory result we report **against** interest — one of three pairs met every part of that criterion (§5). The geometry audit is likewise reported as a floor with a decision procedure, not as another correlation (§6).

**Weaknesses.** The boundary is narrow, and we state it as the boundary of the claims rather than of the paper. §8 states the one separation the design does not make. Two cells carry the epoch-axis intervention, at one budget interval, and the intervention was decided after the primary results; the label axis rests on two pairs; the registered replication is not a clean-protocol result; the density moderator is confounded with the pair; and headroom and domain shift are not separated on the cell that carries the largest gain (§9); and the within-pair budget manipulation of §5 varies the training-image count with the annotation budget, so a gain that grows with the budget there is equally consistent with a small corpus being unable to carry a five-fold learning rate. The backbone swap covers one corpus at one epoch budget, and its ten permutations hold the torch seed fixed. **The searches recorded with this submission did not return a directly corresponding prior measurement.** They did return same-layer neighbours — label statistics used to predict transfer difficulty [9], source-model accuracy used as a transfer predictor [10], isolated backbone comparison in detection [11][12], and architecture-versus-weights separation [34] — and §2, §4.4 and §9 scope the claims against them. What they did not reach is worth stating as well, because it bounds the reading: two channels that cover detection preprints densely, Semantic Scholar without a key and the dblp API, were **unusable in both rounds**, and one round ran with the arXiv API throttled throughout (Appendix O.3). So the positioning is a statement about **what was reachable**, not about what exists.

**How others can benefit.** For a practitioner the transferable reading is asymmetric: at the budgets and resolutions tested, moving the fine-tuning budget changed the sign of a learning-rate gain, while the choice of backbone did not change it materially (§4.4). What travels is a set of **constraints on what may be recorded as a result**, not a recipe that produces effects: a gate fixed before the data decides which diagnostics are allowed to be read as predictors, a split that separates selection from reporting keeps the reported level from being the selecting one, and a magnitude bar frozen in advance decides whether a direction is allowed to be called an effect — and §5 shows what it looks like when the last of these fails. What we do **not** offer is a calibrated threshold: the between-seed SD these instruments divide by is a *data-order* component only, so the bars in this paper are **lower bounds on the noise** and therefore looser than their nominal level.

**Future work.** Three things are unbuilt rather than merely unrun: a design that separates headroom from domain shift on the cell where they covary; a replicated architecture-level contrast on more than one corpus and one epoch budget; and a label axis extended beyond the two pairs that now carry the full grid. The replication's magnitude bar remains unmet on unsaturated targets: the direction replicates, the size does not.
